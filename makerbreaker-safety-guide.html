Taking electronics apart — safely

MakerBreaker is all about curiosity: opening up old gadgets to see how they work and harvesting useful parts. It's great fun and completely safe when you follow a few simple rules. Please read this with a grown-up before the session.

Before you start — the checklist

  • Work with a grown-up nearby, at a clear, well-lit table.
  • Unplug the device. If it ran on the wall, leave it unplugged for the whole session.
  • Take out any batteries and set them aside.
  • Wear eye protection — safety glasses, or even sunglasses — and tie back long hair.
  • Have a small box or bag ready for the parts you harvest, and one for rubbish.

What to bring — and what to leave at home

✅ Great to bring

  • Old radios, toys, remotes, computer mice & keyboards
  • Toy cars, small fans, headphones, wired phones
  • Calculators, alarm clocks, game controllers
  • Anything small that ran on batteries or USB

⛔ Never bring / never open

  • Microwaves — they store a dangerous charge
  • TVs & computer monitors (especially old tube ones)
  • Anything with a big heavy block inside (capacitor)
  • Fluorescent / energy-saver bulbs (contain mercury)
  • Swollen or damaged batteries, vapes

The safe way to take something apart

  1. Make it safe. Unplugged? Batteries out? Good.
  2. Find the screws. Look under stickers, rubber feet and battery covers. Use the right screwdriver — don't force it.
  3. Open gently. Ease the case apart. If it won't budge, look for another screw rather than pulling hard.
  4. Explore & harvest. Spot the motors, speakers, LEDs, wires, switches and gears. Snip wires with the grown-up's help.
  5. Sort & tidy. Keepers in your parts box, sharp bits and rubbish away safely. Wash your hands afterwards.
⚡ Battery rule: never bend, squeeze, poke or heat a battery — especially the flat, pouch-shaped lithium ones in phones and power banks. If a battery is puffy, hot or leaking, don't touch it: tell a grown-up and recycle it at a proper drop-off.

If something goes wrong

  • Small cut or scrape → clean it and use a plaster. Tell your grown-up.
  • A battery gets hot, smokes or smells → move away and let an adult handle it outdoors.
  • Not sure if something is safe to open → don't. Ask first. When in doubt, leave it out.
