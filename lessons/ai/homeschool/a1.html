Prompting Power

Beginner · about 60 minutes · ages 7–10

{{parent}}, please read first: Most AI chat tools require users to be 13+ (or 18+). For this age group, you should operate the account and keyboard while {{child}} directs what to ask. Keep the session supervised and use a kid-safe or general assistant you're comfortable with.

1. Session Overview

{{child}} learns the single most useful AI skill: writing a good prompt (the instructions we give an AI). By comparing weak and strong prompts side by side, {{child}} discovers that clear, detailed requests get far better answers — a skill that helps with AI, with search engines, and with asking anyone for help.

2. Learning Objectives

By the end {{child}} will be able to:

  • Explain what a prompt is and why detail matters.
  • Improve a vague prompt by adding who / what / how.
  • Judge whether an AI answer is helpful or needs a follow-up.
  • Understand that AI can be confidently wrong.

3. Materials & Setup

  • A computer or tablet with internet.
  • A parent-operated AI assistant account.
  • Paper and pencil for {{child}} to plan prompts.

4. Flexible Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • Warm-up (10 min): Play "give me instructions" — {{child}} tells {{parent}} exactly how to make a sandwich. Vague steps = funny results!
  • Explore (20 min): Try weak vs. strong prompts and compare answers.
  • Practice (20 min): {{child}} improves three prompts of their own.
  • Reflect (10 min): What made the best answers?

5. Activity Breakdown

Try these prompt pairs together

Type the weak one, read the answer, then the strong one, and compare:

  • Weak: "Tell me about dogs."
    Strong: "Explain to a 7-year-old why dogs wag their tails, in 3 short sentences."
  • Weak: "Write a story."
    Strong: "Write a 5-sentence bedtime story about a brave tomato who is scared of the dark. Make it funny."
  • Weak: "Help with maths."
    Strong: "Give me 3 addition problems for a 7-year-old, then show the answers separately."

The recipe for a strong prompt

Help {{child}} remember three ingredients: Who it's for, What you want, and How (length, style, funny/serious).

Success looks like: {{child}} can take a boring prompt and add detail to make the answer clearly better.

6. Conversation Starters & Check-ins

  • "Which answer did you like more? What made it better?"
  • "What could we add to get an even better reply?"
  • "Do you think the AI is always right? How could we check?"

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Play "prompt telephone": {{child}} keeps improving one prompt three times.
  • Ask the AI to explain the same thing to a baby, then to a scientist — compare.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • Answer is too long/complex: add "in 3 short sentences for a child".
  • AI makes something up: great teaching moment — check the fact together in a book or trusted site.

9. Parent Guidance Tips

{{parent}}, resist typing your own better prompt — coach {{child}} to improve theirs by asking "who is it for?" and "how long should it be?". Model healthy skepticism: treat AI as a clever but sometimes-mistaken helper, not an oracle.

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Keep a "prompt journal" of {{child}}'s best prompts and the answers they got.
