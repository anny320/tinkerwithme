Build Your Portfolio

Advanced · about 120 minutes · ages 13–16

{{parent}}, please read first: This capstone may involve creating a simple online profile (e.g. a GitHub account, 13+ with parental consent) or an offline portfolio document. Supervise any account creation and privacy settings; an offline slide deck works just as well.

1. Session Overview

The AI track finale: {{child}} gathers their best work from earlier lessons and presents it as a real portfolio and short pitch. This teaches how creators showcase and talk about their work — a genuinely valuable life skill for school, competitions and future opportunities. {{child}} finishes with something concrete to be proud of.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Select and organise their strongest projects.
  • Write clear descriptions of what they made and learned.
  • Build a simple portfolio (online profile or slide deck).
  • Deliver a short, confident pitch about their work.

3. Materials & Setup

  • Computer/tablet with internet.
  • {{child}}'s saved work from earlier lessons (stories, images, models, notes).
  • A slideshow app, document, or a supervised GitHub/profile account.

4. Flexible Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • Gather (20 min): Collect {{child}}'s best pieces in one folder.
  • Curate (25 min): Pick the top 3–5 and write a line about each.
  • Build (45 min): Assemble the portfolio (deck or profile).
  • Pitch prep & deliver (30 min): Write and practise a 1-minute pitch; present it.

5. Activity Breakdown

Step 1 — Curate

For each chosen project, {{child}} writes: what it is, how they made it, and what they learned.

Step 2 — Build the portfolio

  • Offline option: a clean slide deck — one project per slide, with an image and the three lines.
  • Online option (supervised): a simple GitHub profile README or a free portfolio page. Keep personal details private.

Step 3 — Write a pitch

A 1-minute intro: "Hi, I'm {{child}}. This year I learned to [skills]. My favourite project was [X] because [reason]. Here's what I want to build next."

Step 4 — Present

{{child}} delivers the pitch to {{parent}} (and ideally other family) and answers a couple of questions.

Success looks like: a tidy portfolio of 3–5 projects and {{child}} delivering a clear, confident one-minute pitch about their work.

6. Conversation Starters & Check-ins

  • "Which project are you proudest of, and why?"
  • "If someone saw only this portfolio, what would they learn about you?"
  • "What do you want to make next?"

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Add a short "about me" and a goal for next year.
  • Record the pitch as a video to keep and improve later.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • Too much to include: quality over quantity — 3 strong pieces beat 10 weak ones.
  • Nervous about pitching: practise twice; use cue cards; keep it to one minute.

9. Parent Guidance Tips

{{parent}}, this is a celebration of {{child}}'s whole journey — treat it like a real showcase. Be an encouraging audience, ask genuine questions, and help with privacy on anything online. The confidence {{child}} builds presenting their work will outlast any single project.

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Share the portfolio with a mentor, teacher, or the TinkerWithMe community for feedback.
  • Set one goal for the next thing {{child}} wants to learn or build.
