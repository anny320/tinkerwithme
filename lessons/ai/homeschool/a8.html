AI Careers Deep Dive

Intermediate · about 60 minutes · ages 13–16

{{parent}}, please read first: This is a discussion-and-research lesson. Any AI tool use is parent-supervised and optional. It pairs well with your own family knowledge and local context.

1. Session Overview

{{child}} explores the growing world of AI careers — not just "programmer", but ethicists, data scientists, designers, researchers, and roles in fields like medicine, farming and art. The goal is to broaden {{child}}'s sense of possibility and connect AI to their own interests and to opportunities in their community.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Name several distinct roles in the AI field and what each does.
  • Connect AI to careers beyond "tech" (health, agriculture, art, law).
  • Identify skills these roles need — including non-coding ones.
  • Relate AI careers to {{child}}'s own interests.

3. Materials & Setup

  • Computer/tablet for research (parent-supervised).
  • Notebook for a "careers map".

4. Flexible Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • Warm-up (10 min): List every job {{child}} thinks involves AI. Longer than expected?
  • Explore roles (25 min): Look at the roles below; note what each does day-to-day.
  • Connect (20 min): Match {{child}}'s interests to a role.
  • Reflect (5 min): One role {{child}} would like to try.

5. Activity Breakdown

Roles to explore

RoleWhat they doA key skill
ML EngineerBuilds and trains AI modelsCoding & maths
Data ScientistFinds patterns and meaning in dataCuriosity & statistics
AI EthicistMakes sure AI is fair and safeCritical thinking
Prompt/UX DesignerMakes AI easy and pleasant to useEmpathy & communication
Data Labeller/CuratorPrepares quality examples for trainingAttention to detail
Domain Expert (e.g. doctor, farmer)Guides AI in their fieldDeep field knowledge

Research prompt (optional)

"Explain what an AI ethicist does in a normal week, for a 14-year-old, in 5 bullet points." — then fact-check the answer together.

Success looks like: {{child}} can describe at least three different AI careers and link one to something they personally enjoy.

6. Conversation Starters & Check-ins

  • "Which of these jobs surprised you? Why?"
  • "Do you need to be a coder to work with AI? What else matters?"
  • "How could AI help in a job that matters in our community?"

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Interview a family member about how AI could help their work.
  • Design {{child}}'s dream AI job that doesn't exist yet.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • Feels abstract: anchor each role to a concrete example ("an AI ethicist checks the Bias Detective stuff we found").

9. Parent Guidance Tips

{{parent}}, emphasise that AI is a field for many kinds of thinkers — not only mathematicians. Connect roles to {{child}}'s strengths, whether that's art, writing, fairness, or organisation. Keep it aspirational and grounded in real, local possibilities.

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Make a one-page "future me" poster for an AI-related role {{child}} likes.
