AI Storytelling

Beginner · about 75 minutes · ages 10–13

{{parent}}, please read first: Use a parent-supervised AI assistant (and image generator, if illustrating). For under-13s, operate the account yourself while {{child}} leads the storytelling.

1. Session Overview

{{child}} co-writes an illustrated story with AI — but as the director, not the passenger. {{child}} invents characters and plot, uses AI to draft and expand, then edits and improves. The key lesson: AI is a writing partner, and the human is in charge of the ideas and the final polish.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Plan a story with characters, setting and a problem to solve.
  • Use AI to draft, then revise its output critically.
  • Give feedback prompts ("make it funnier", "shorter", "add a twist").
  • Combine text and AI images into a finished piece.

3. Materials & Setup

  • Computer/tablet with a parent-operated AI assistant.
  • Optional: an image generator for illustrations.
  • Notebook for planning.

4. Flexible Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • Plan (15 min): {{child}} decides the hero, setting and problem on paper first.
  • Draft (20 min): Use AI to expand the plan into a first draft.
  • Revise (25 min): Improve it with feedback prompts and {{child}}'s own edits.
  • Illustrate & finish (15 min): Add pictures and a title.

5. Activity Breakdown

Step 1 — Plan (on paper, {{child}}'s ideas)

Hero, sidekick, setting, the problem, how it's solved.

Step 2 — Draft prompt

"Write the first part of a story for a 10-year-old about [hero] who [problem] in [setting]. Stop after the problem appears so we can decide what happens next."

Step 3 — Revision prompts

  • "Make the hero braver and add a funny sidekick line."
  • "This part is confusing — rewrite it more simply."
  • "Add a surprising twist before the ending."

Step 4 — {{child}}'s own touch

{{child}} rewrites at least the ending in their own words.

Success looks like: a finished story where {{child}} directed the plot and improved the AI's draft — not just accepted it.

6. Conversation Starters & Check-ins

  • "Which parts are your ideas and which did the AI suggest?"
  • "What did you change, and why was your version better?"
  • "How would this story be different if only the AI wrote it?"

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Turn the story into a comic with AI illustrations per scene.
  • Write a sequel starring the sidekick.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • AI takes over the plot: tell it to stop and ask {{child}} what happens next each time.
  • Draft feels generic: add specific details from {{child}}'s plan (names, a special object).

9. Parent Guidance Tips

{{parent}}, the goal is authorship, not automation. Keep {{child}} making the creative decisions and always require an original edit. Praise the revising as much as the writing — critical reading of AI output is the real 21st-century skill here.

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Read the finished story aloud to family; discuss what {{child}} would change next time.
