AI Art Studio

Beginner · about 60 minutes · ages 7–10

{{parent}}, please read first: Image generators have age limits and can occasionally produce unexpected results. You should operate the tool and preview every image. Use a family-friendly image generator you trust, and keep prompts wholesome.

1. Session Overview

{{child}} becomes a digital artist, turning words into pictures with an AI image generator. The lesson focuses on describing — colour, style, mood, setting — so {{child}} learns that the richer the description, the better the artwork. It's imagination made visible.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Describe an image with subject, setting, style and colours.
  • Iterate: change the prompt to change the picture.
  • Compare art styles (cartoon, watercolour, pixel art).
  • Talk about how AI images are made from patterns, not photos.

3. Materials & Setup

  • A computer/tablet with internet and a parent-run image generator.
  • A notebook for {{child}} to sketch ideas first.

4. Flexible Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • Warm-up (10 min): {{child}} describes a made-up creature in words; {{parent}} draws it. Detail helps!
  • Create (25 min): Generate images from {{child}}'s descriptions.
  • Restyle (15 min): Take one idea and try three art styles.
  • Gallery (10 min): Pick a favourite and say why.

5. Activity Breakdown

Build a picture prompt together

Use this fill-in frame with {{child}}:

"A [adjective] [animal or character], [doing something], in a [place], [art style], [colours]."

  • Example: "A friendly purple dragon reading a book in a cozy library, cartoon style, warm colours."
  • Example: "A tiny robot watering flowers on the moon, watercolour, soft pastel colours."

Try style words

cartoon · watercolour · pixel art · crayon drawing · claymation · comic book

Success looks like: {{child}} changes one part of the description (colour or style) and can predict how the picture will change.

6. Conversation Starters & Check-ins

  • "Which words changed the picture the most?"
  • "Is the AI copying a real photo, or making something new?"
  • "How is this different from you drawing it yourself?"

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Illustrate a character for a story {{child}} is writing.
  • Make a "before and after" — the same idea in two very different styles.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • Picture ignores part of the prompt: put the most important detail first and try again.
  • Looks odd (hands, faces): normal for AI — a fun chance to talk about what AI finds hard.

9. Parent Guidance Tips

{{parent}}, preview each image before {{child}} sees it. Emphasise that AI art is a tool for imagination, not a replacement for {{child}}'s own drawing — do a hand-drawn version too. Keep it playful and celebrate the funny "mistakes".

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Print a favourite image and have {{child}} redraw it by hand.
  • Make a little illustrated poster combining several creations.
