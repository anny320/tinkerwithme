Bias Detective

Intermediate · about 75 minutes · ages 13–16

{{parent}}, please read first: This lesson explores how AI can be unfair. Use parent-supervised tools and preview outputs. Some results may reflect real-world stereotypes — that's the point of discussion, but stay nearby to guide the conversation.

1. Session Overview

{{child}} investigates a serious side of AI: bias. Because AI learns from human-made data, it can absorb and repeat unfair assumptions. {{child}} will run experiments to surface bias, reason about where it comes from, and discuss what fairness in technology means. This builds critical thinking and digital citizenship.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Explain that AI learns from data, and biased data creates biased AI.
  • Design simple tests that reveal bias.
  • Evaluate AI output critically rather than accepting it.
  • Discuss fairness, representation and real-world consequences.

3. Materials & Setup

  • Computer/tablet with a parent-supervised AI text and/or image tool.
  • Notebook to record findings like a real researcher.

4. Flexible Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • Warm-up (10 min): What does "fair" mean? When has {{child}} felt something was unfair?
  • Investigate (30 min): Run the bias tests below and record results.
  • Analyse (25 min): Where might the bias come from? Who could it hurt?
  • Propose (10 min): How could we make AI fairer?

5. Activity Breakdown

Experiments (record what you notice)

  • Job test: ask for images or descriptions of "a doctor", "a nurse", "a scientist", "a cleaner". Who shows up? Any patterns in gender, age, or appearance?
  • Story test: "Write a story about a brilliant engineer." Note the character's traits. Repeat a few times.
  • Assumption test: "Describe a typical family." What's included — and what's left out?

Detective's questions

  • What did the AI assume without being told?
  • Where did it learn that assumption?
  • Who might be misrepresented or excluded?
Success looks like: {{child}} can point to a specific biased output and explain, in their own words, why it's unfair and where it likely came from.

6. Conversation Starters & Check-ins

  • "If AI decided who gets a job or a loan, why would bias be dangerous?"
  • "Whose voices might be missing from the data?"
  • "Should we trust AI to be neutral? Why or why not?"

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Try to "correct" a biased prompt by adding detail — does it help or just paper over it?
  • Research a real news story about AI bias and summarise it.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • Tool refuses a prompt: good — discuss why guardrails exist; rephrase to explore respectfully.
  • No obvious bias appears: try more examples; bias is often about patterns across many outputs.

9. Parent Guidance Tips

{{parent}}, this is a values conversation as much as a tech one. Stay engaged and let {{child}} reach conclusions through evidence. Reinforce that noticing bias isn't about "AI being bad" — it's about being a thoughtful, responsible user and future builder of technology.

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Write a short "fairness checklist" {{child}} would give to people who build AI.
