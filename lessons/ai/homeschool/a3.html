AI Music & Sound

Beginner · about 60 minutes · ages 7–10

{{parent}}, please read first: Use a family-friendly, parent-operated AI music tool. You run the account; {{child}} directs the creativity. Preview generated audio before sharing.

1. Session Overview

{{child}} composes music with AI — describing a mood, style and instruments and hearing the AI bring it to life. The lesson builds the vocabulary of music (tempo, mood, instruments) and shows how the same description ideas from art and writing apply to sound too.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Describe music by mood, tempo, instruments and style.
  • Iterate a prompt to change how a piece feels.
  • Recognise how music sets a scene (happy, spooky, epic).
  • Discuss how AI learns patterns from lots of existing music.

3. Materials & Setup

  • Computer/tablet with speakers or headphones.
  • A parent-operated AI music generator.

4. Flexible Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • Warm-up (10 min): Play short clips; {{child}} guesses the mood (scary? happy? sleepy?).
  • Create (25 min): Generate music for different moods.
  • Soundtrack a scene (15 min): Make music for a "chase" and a "calm morning".
  • Share (10 min): Play favourites and describe them.

5. Activity Breakdown

Music prompt frame

"[Mood] music for [a scene], [tempo: slow/medium/fast], with [instruments], in a [style]."

  • "Happy, bouncy music for a birthday party, fast tempo, with ukulele and claps, cartoon style."
  • "Spooky, mysterious music for a haunted house, slow tempo, with low piano and strings."
  • "Epic adventure music for a hero entering a castle, medium tempo, with drums and trumpets."
Success looks like: {{child}} can change the mood word and hear the music's feeling change to match.

6. Conversation Starters & Check-ins

  • "How does this music make you feel? Which instrument does that?"
  • "If this played in a film, what would be happening on screen?"
  • "Where do you think the AI learned to make music like this?"

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Make a theme song for {{child}}, or for a pet.
  • Create two contrasting tracks and switch between them to tell a mini story.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • Music doesn't match the mood: lead with the mood word and name specific instruments.
  • Too long/short: most tools let you set a length — adjust and retry.

9. Parent Guidance Tips

{{parent}}, connect this to real listening — point out soundtracks in the films and games {{child}} already loves. Encourage describing feelings in words; that emotional vocabulary is valuable well beyond this lesson.

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Pair an AI song with an AI picture from the Art Studio lesson for a "mood card".
