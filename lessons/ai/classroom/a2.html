AI Art Studio

Beginner · about 60 minutes · ages 7–10 · classroom

{{parent}}, please read first: Image generators have age limits and can produce unexpected results. Run the tool on a facilitator device and preview every image before showing the class. Keep prompts wholesome.

1. Session Overview

Students turn words into pictures with an AI image generator, focusing on rich description — subject, setting, style, colour. The class learns that better descriptions make better art, while comparing styles.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Describe an image with subject, setting, style, colours.
  • Iterate a prompt to change the result.
  • Compare art styles.
  • Discuss that AI images come from patterns, not photos.

3. Materials & Setup

  • Facilitator device + projector with a family-friendly image generator.
  • Notebooks for sketching ideas first.

4. Minute-by-Minute Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • 0:00–0:10 — Hook: a student describes a creature; class draws it — detail helps.
  • 0:10–0:35 — Create: groups submit descriptions; facilitator generates and displays.
  • 0:35–0:50 — Restyle: one idea in three styles.
  • 0:50–1:00 — Gallery walk & vote.

5. Activity Breakdown

Picture-prompt frame

"A [adjective] [character], [action], in a [place], [art style], [colours]."

  • "A friendly purple dragon reading in a cozy library, cartoon style, warm colours."
  • "A tiny robot watering flowers on the moon, watercolour, soft pastels."

Style words: cartoon · watercolour · pixel art · crayon · claymation · comic.

Success criteria: a group changes one element (colour/style) and predicts the change.

6. Assessment

  • Each group explains which words changed their picture most.
  • Discussion: "is the AI copying a photo or making something new?"

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Illustrate a class-story character; do a "same idea, two styles" comparison.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • Prompt partly ignored: put the key detail first.
  • Odd hands/faces: discuss what AI finds hard.

9. Classroom Management Tips

{{parent}}, previewing on one facilitator screen keeps it safe and paced. Have groups plan on paper and hand up prompts so generation time doesn't create idle waiting — assign the wait as sketching-by-hand time.

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Students redraw a favourite AI image by hand for a display board.
