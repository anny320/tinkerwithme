AI Storytelling

Beginner · about 75 minutes · ages 10–13 · classroom

{{parent}}, please read first: Use parent/school-approved AI tools. For under-13s, prefer a facilitator device or supervised school accounts. Preview outputs.

1. Session Overview

Students co-write an illustrated story with AI as the director, not the passenger. They invent characters and plot, use AI to draft and expand, then critically edit and improve. Core lesson: AI is a writing partner; the human owns the ideas and the final polish.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Plan a story (characters, setting, problem).
  • Use AI to draft, then revise critically.
  • Give feedback prompts (funnier, shorter, add a twist).
  • Combine text and images into a finished piece.

3. Materials & Setup

  • Supervised devices (or facilitator device); optional image generator.
  • Story-planning worksheet.

4. Minute-by-Minute Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • 0:00–0:15 — Plan: groups choose hero, setting, problem on paper.
  • 0:15–0:35 — Draft: AI expands the plan into a first draft.
  • 0:35–1:00 — Revise: feedback prompts + students' own edits.
  • 1:00–1:15 — Illustrate, title, share.

5. Activity Breakdown

Draft prompt

"Write the first part of a story for a 10-year-old about [hero] who [problem] in [setting]. Stop after the problem appears."

Revision prompts

  • "Make the hero braver; add a funny sidekick line."
  • "Rewrite this part more simply."
  • "Add a surprising twist before the ending."

Own touch

Each group rewrites at least the ending in their own words.

Success criteria: a finished story the group directed and improved — not just accepted.

6. Assessment

  • Groups annotate which parts are theirs vs AI-suggested.
  • Peer review: swap stories and give one improvement.

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Turn the story into a comic; write a sequel starring the sidekick.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • AI takes over the plot: tell it to stop and ask the group what happens next.
  • Generic draft: add specific details from the plan.

9. Classroom Management Tips

{{parent}}, require an original edit from every group so authorship stays with students. Praise revising as much as drafting. If devices are limited, run drafts on a facilitator screen and have groups revise on paper before finalising.

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Author's chair: groups read finished stories aloud and discuss their choices.
