Bias Detective

Intermediate · about 75 minutes · ages 13–16 · classroom

{{parent}}, please read first: This explores how AI can be unfair; some outputs may reflect real stereotypes — that's the discussion point. Use supervised tools, preview outputs, and facilitate the conversation actively.

1. Session Overview

Students investigate bias in AI. Because AI learns from human-made data, it can absorb unfair assumptions. Groups run experiments to surface bias, reason about its origins, and discuss fairness — building critical thinking and digital citizenship.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Explain that biased data creates biased AI.
  • Design simple tests that reveal bias.
  • Evaluate AI output critically.
  • Discuss fairness, representation and consequences.

3. Materials & Setup

  • Supervised AI text/image tools (facilitator device works well).
  • A findings worksheet per group.

4. Minute-by-Minute Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • 0:00–0:10 — Hook: what does "fair" mean? when has something felt unfair?
  • 0:10–0:40 — Investigate: run the bias tests; record results.
  • 0:40–1:05 — Analyse: where does the bias come from? who's harmed?
  • 1:05–1:15 — Propose fairness ideas; share.

5. Activity Breakdown

Experiments (record findings)

  • Job test: images/descriptions of "a doctor", "a nurse", "a scientist", "a cleaner" — note patterns.
  • Story test: "Write about a brilliant engineer." Note the character; repeat.
  • Assumption test: "Describe a typical family." What's included/left out?

Detective questions

  • What did the AI assume unprompted? Where did it learn that? Who might be excluded?
Success criteria: a group points to a specific biased output and explains why it's unfair and its likely origin.

6. Assessment

  • Each group presents one documented example of bias with analysis.
  • Debate: "should we trust AI to be neutral?"

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Try to "correct" a biased prompt — does it fix or just mask the issue?
  • Summarise a real news story about AI bias.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • Tool refuses a prompt: discuss why guardrails exist; rephrase respectfully.
  • No obvious bias: gather more examples — bias shows in patterns.

9. Classroom Management Tips

{{parent}}, this is a values discussion as much as a tech one — set a respectful tone and let evidence drive conclusions. Facilitate actively so findings stay constructive. Frame it as becoming responsible users and future builders, not "AI is bad".

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Class drafts a shared "fairness checklist" for people who build AI.
