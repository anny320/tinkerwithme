Build Your Portfolio

Advanced · about 120 minutes · ages 13–16 · classroom

{{parent}}, please read first: May involve creating a simple online profile (e.g. GitHub, 13+ with consent) or an offline portfolio. Supervise account creation and privacy settings; an offline deck works just as well.

1. Session Overview

The AI-track capstone: students gather their best work from earlier lessons and present it as a real portfolio with a short pitch. They learn to showcase and talk about their work — a valuable life skill for school, competitions and opportunities — and finish with something concrete to be proud of.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Select and organise their strongest projects.
  • Write clear descriptions of what they made and learned.
  • Build a simple portfolio (profile or deck).
  • Deliver a short, confident pitch.

3. Materials & Setup

  • Devices with internet; students' saved work from earlier lessons.
  • Slideshow app, or supervised profile accounts.

4. Minute-by-Minute Schedule (planned session: {{duration}})

  • 0:00–0:20 — Gather: collect each student's best pieces.
  • 0:20–0:45 — Curate: pick top 3–5; write a line about each.
  • 0:45–1:30 — Build: assemble the portfolio.
  • 1:30–2:00 — Pitch prep & showcase: practise, then present.

5. Activity Breakdown

Step 1 — Curate

For each chosen project: what it is, how they made it, what they learned.

Step 2 — Build

  • Offline: a clean slide deck, one project per slide.
  • Online (supervised): a simple profile README — keep personal details private.

Step 3 — Pitch (1 minute)

"Hi, I'm [student]. This term I learned [skills]. My favourite project was [X] because [reason]. Next I want to build [Y]."

Success criteria: a tidy 3–5 project portfolio and a clear one-minute pitch.

6. Assessment

  • Showcase presentations, peer-assessed on clarity and pride of work.
  • Rubric: selection, descriptions, delivery.

7. Extensions & Challenges

  • Add an "about me" and a next-term goal; record the pitch as a video.

8. Troubleshooting Guide

  • Too much to include: quality over quantity — 3 strong pieces.
  • Pitch nerves: practise twice; cue cards; keep to one minute.

9. Classroom Management Tips

{{parent}}, run this as a genuine showcase — invite another class or families as an audience. Timebox the build so students reach the pitch. Help with privacy settings on anything online. Celebrate the whole journey, not just polished projects.

10. Follow-up Activities

  • Share portfolios with mentors or the TinkerWithMe community for feedback; set a next goal.
